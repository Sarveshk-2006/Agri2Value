# 09 — Demo, Pitch and Judging Strategy

## 9.1 The story judges should remember

Many agricultural tools provide one piece of information: a price, a crop advisory, a buyer listing or a weather update. Agri2Value connects the farmer's decision to the action and the final financial outcome.

**Core message:** Agri2Value helps a farmer choose the best feasible value path for a harvest and follow it through to actual profit.

Do not claim that no other platform offers crop intelligence, mandi data, irrigation or marketplaces. The defensible differentiator is the integrated, feasibility-aware decision-to-execution workflow and the proof of its operation.

## 9.2 Four-minute demo script

Adapt the timings to the event's actual rules.

### 0:00–0:25 — Introduce the farmer's problem

“Imagine a farmer who has harvested 1,000 kg of tomatoes. The local market gives one price, but another buyer or a processing route may offer a better return. The farmer cannot choose based on price alone. Transport, processing, buyer availability, time and risk all matter.”

### 0:25–0:55 — Enter the harvest

Show farmer login, farm profile and crop lot. Explain which values are farmer-entered and which are demo data.

### 0:55–1:45 — Show the Profit Decision Engine

Display raw sale, alternative buyer, processing and collective-sale scenarios. Open the cost breakdown. Point out processing yield, transport and packaging costs. Show feasibility status and why the top option ranks first.

### 1:45–2:25 — Turn recommendation into execution

Select the recommended option. Request/confirm buyer and transport. Show the “ready to execute” conditions. If provider availability is simulated, say so clearly.

### 2:25–3:00 — Show Crop Rescue Mode

Cancel the first buyer or trigger an urgent scenario. Recalculate feasible alternatives. Show the new option and the required actions. Explain that it helps prioritize and coordinate recovery; it does not guarantee a buyer.

### 3:00–3:35 — Protect and verify

Show agreement/offer details, delivery quantity, actual costs, payment status and actual-profit reconciliation.

### 3:35–4:00 — Close with impact and roadmap

“Agri2Value connects understanding, decision, execution and verification. Our next step is to validate the model with real farmers, buyers and processors in a defined pilot geography.”

## 9.3 Short pitch draft

Agri2Value is an AI-assisted farm-to-profit decision and execution platform. Farmers often see market prices or receive separate crop advisories, but they still have to decide what to do with their harvest and arrange the buyer, processing, transport and other resources themselves.

Agri2Value brings those decisions together. A farmer enters a crop lot, and our Profit Decision Engine compares realistic options such as raw sale, direct buyer, processing and collective selling. It calculates expected net return after costs and checks whether each option is actually feasible. The farmer can then request the buyer and logistics needed to execute the selected path, track the transaction and compare expected profit with what was actually received.

Our proposed Crop Rescue Mode handles urgent situations such as a buyer cancellation or a harvest at risk by re-evaluating available alternatives. Farmer Protection adds contract explanations, transaction evidence and payment tracking.

Our focus is not another disconnected agriculture dashboard. It is a complete decision-to-execution loop that helps farmers make more informed, feasible and transparent crop-value decisions.

## 9.4 Judge questions to prepare for

### “What is actually innovative?”

The individual technologies are established. Our proposed innovation is their integration around a feasibility-aware net-profit decision and execution loop: the system does not stop at an advisory but checks the resources and transaction conditions required to act, then records the actual result.

### “How do you calculate profit?”

Show the formula and every cost line. Explain processing yield, saleable quantity, buyer offer status, transport and other costs. Show the input snapshot and the calculation version. Be ready to reproduce the number manually.

### “Where does your data come from?”

For each displayed value, identify whether it is farmer-entered, live sourced, provider quoted, sensor measured, model estimated or demo data. Show timestamps. Never describe seeded values as live.

### “How do you know the processing option is possible?”

We check processor location, capacity, input quality, timing, yield/cost assumptions and buyer/demand path. If not confirmed, the option is conditional or infeasible, not a guaranteed recommendation.

### “How do you protect farmers?”

We show contract terms that may need clarification, preserve accepted offer and delivery records, and track payment status. We do not replace a lawyer or guarantee legal protection.

### “How is Crop Rescue different?”

It is an urgent re-planning workflow that prioritizes time, perishability, feasibility and recoverable net value after an event such as cancellation or delay. It coordinates alternatives rather than promising to save every crop.

### “How will you scale?”

The architecture is modular. Start with one crop and pilot geography, validate with farmers and buyers, then add crops, markets, processors and FPO workflows using the same data and decision model with crop-specific parameters.

### “How will you prove impact?”

Run a pilot and measure time to decision, feasible-option execution rate, realized net return, estimate-vs-actual error, spoilage/rejection and farmer comprehension. Report sample size and method; do not promise uplift before evidence.

## 9.5 Evidence pack to bring

- Architecture diagram.
- One end-to-end workflow diagram.
- Formula sheet and worked example.
- Test report for profit calculations.
- Data source list with timestamps and terms.
- AI evaluation report if disease detection is included.
- ESP32 hardware photo and telemetry trace if IoT is included.
- Screenshots of buyer/transport confirmation and transaction reconciliation.
- Demo limitations and future roadmap.

## 9.6 Presentation rules

- Lead with the farmer's decision, not the technology stack.
- Explain one farmer scenario from start to finish.
- Keep the product name and terms consistent.
- Use one primary crop in the demo.
- Do not claim “100% accurate”, “guaranteed profit”, “eliminates middlemen” or “prevents all losses”.
- Separate working features from prototype simulations and future scope.
- Demonstrate a calculation, not just a chart.
- End with the measurable farmer outcome and validation plan.
